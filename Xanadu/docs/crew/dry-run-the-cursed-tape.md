# Crew dry run: The Cursed Tape (Guess The Horror #1)

Phase 1 proof. The crew ran on paper over an episode that is already finished, so Chria can read how each role thinks before any code is built. The script is the existing `spec.js` (PS1 v2, 13 lines, about 71 s). Nothing here was rendered.

## Director: treatment
- Tone in three words: damp, patient, inevitable.
- References for the look: a 2002 Pacific Northwest drizzle (cold teal-green, lifted blacks), the grey-blue of a CRT in a dark room, the chalky white of a cassette label.
- Colour mood by act: Act 1 (the tape, the call) cyan and TV grey; Act 2 (the investigation, the cabin, the well) green-black with one warm lantern; Act 3 (the seventh night) blue-black with the TV as the only light.
- Pacing: slow, locked-off frames for the first 20 s so the viewer leans in; the camera starts moving at the cabin; handheld only for the crawl. One hit, at the TV turning on.
- Hidden clue: the ring of light on the TV after "a burning tree", half a second, never narrated.
- Last beat before the loop: the wet-haired figure crawling out of the screen, then the case snapping shut, back to the title card.
- Blocking in one line per scene: the reporter sits on the couch facing the TV (we never see her face clearly); the ex-husband stands at the window of a bare apartment; the boy in the doorway; the cabin floorboards are pulled up from camera left; the well is shot straight down.

## Script supervisor: checks
All ten checks pass on paper. Two notes: S6B's hair must be re-checked after the motion library lands (the crawl is the hardest pose); S1A and S6C must share the set, light and camera for the loop seam.

## Editor: cut notes
- Frame 1 is the closed case with the title card; the case opens in the first second, under the narration.
- Three hard cuts on the three tape pictures, then the ring of light held for 0.5 s with the music dropping out.
- The clock jump is cut to the tick; the TV turns on exactly on the word "itself"; the crawl is the longest shot, 2.3 s, held through the whisper "Seven days."
- Dip to black on "this...?", the case snaps shut, 0.4 s hold on the title card, loop. Target 71 s.

## Sound engineer: mix notes
- Existing cues stay (rain, static, VCR clunk, phone, the heartbeats, the crawl). New foley from the motion library: floorboards on each step in S4, the lid grinding on the stone.
- Beds: the rain bed under S1 and S3 only; wind under S4 and S5; the crescendo bed from the TV turning on to the end, cut dead on the dip to black.
- Rooms: S1 small carpeted (dry), S4 wooden (short slap), S5 deep stone (long echo on the drip and the whisper).
- -14 LUFS, true peak -1 dB. The song slot for the post kit: 0:37 to 0:41 (over the woods walk).

## Colorist: grade
- Power grade: 2002 Cyan. Lift +0.02 blue, gain 0.92 red, saturation 0.85, contrast 1.12, temperature -0.3.
- Per act: Act 2 drifts 10 percent toward green; Act 3 pulls saturation to 0.7 and lets the TV's blue-white blow out by one stop.
- Film layer: large-format fine grain, halation 0.3 on the TV and the ring, print curve on, no weave. S5B (the flashback) switches to the Super 8 Home Movie grade with weave and a light leak.
- Vibrancy ceiling 1.15. No letterbox.

## Producer
Take numbering continues from PS1 Take 03. Raw plates for 71 s at 1080p, 3 passes: about 12 GB on the T7. Post kit updated with the song slot above.

## What this dry run showed
- The roles hand off cleanly in this order: writer, director (treatment, blocking), casting and production designer in parallel, gaffer, cinematographer, script supervisor, shoot, editor, sound engineer and colorist in parallel, producer.
- Two things the libraries need before the real run: a waist-length veil hair asset and a crawl pose, both already on the Phase 3 list.
- The gaffer's "stops" and the cinematographer's "exposure" must use the same scale. Phase 2 defines it once in `lib/lights.js`, and the camera reads it.
